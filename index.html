How Pension Planner works

Start by entering the household, income sources, pensions and assets. Pension Planner then applies growth, tax, withdrawal and sequencing logic year by year and turns the result into decision-support outputs.

Typical workflow

  • Create one or two people and set key dates such as retirement and State Pension age.
  • Add pensions, investments, GIAs, annuities, rental income and other recurring income.
  • Set spending, inflation, growth assumptions and any one-off capital events.
  • Review Forecast, Dashboard, Annual Detail and Model Health.
  • Duplicate scenarios to compare alternatives such as different retirement dates or withdrawal strategies.

One model for the main retirement decisions

Pension Planner is designed as a what-if laboratory rather than a black-box recommendation engine. Build a base case, understand the mechanics, then duplicate the scenario and change one decision at a time.

Understanding the tax-efficient withdrawal strategy

Retirement spending is a household requirement, but UK tax belongs to individuals. Pension Planner therefore keeps ownership explicit and compares the tax consequences of drawing from different people and different account types.

What the strategy tries to do

  • Use available tax-free or low-tax funding capacity sensibly.
  • Take account of both partners rather than treating the household as one taxpayer.
  • Recognise that pensions, ISA/cash capital and GIA withdrawals have different tax consequences.
  • Avoid wasting useful tax allowances where that improves the net outcome for the household.

General Investment Account modelling

Pension Planner models a GIA explicitly, including annual dividends, pooled acquisition cost, disposals, realised gains and modelled Capital Gains Tax.
